# KIIKIO — Creative Direction
**Phase 2 deliverable · Art direction, world, and brand voice**
*Builds on the Phase 1 audit.*

---

## 1. The big idea

> **AFTER THE STORM.**

Three years ago Kiikio's founding story begins on a stormy night, a single hoodie on a sewing table. The Lightning collection is already named after this moment. We make this myth the **operating principle** of the entire brand.

Every Kiikio piece is an **artifact that survived something** — distressed, washed, stitched back together, weathered and intact. Every collection is a **chapter** of the storm cycle. Every drop is a **dispatch from after the weather changed**.

**Why this works:**
- It tells the truth about the product (the distressing, washing, hardware, deconstruction is *intentional weathering*, not a flaw).
- It justifies the dark, dust-and-storm photography that already exists.
- It works at Kiikio's price point — survival is not luxury, it's earned.
- It gives every season a free narrative engine ("Chapter II: Aftermath", "Chapter III: Dustline").
- It's ownable. Hellstar has the goth-religion lane. Carsicko has the UK gothic lane. Broken Planet has sustainability. *Storm-survivor-mythos* is open.

**One-line brand voice:**
> "Kiikio. After the storm."

## 2. Mood board direction (verbal — visual moodboard to be assembled in Phase 5)

Six visual columns the redesign draws from:

1. **Storm light** — golden-hour desert dust, post-rain wet asphalt, lightning silhouettes, single-source side light. Reference: Hiroshi Sugimoto seascapes, Gregory Crewdson stills, Carsicko campaign films.
2. **Found materials** — raw linen tags, kraft paper, stamped ink, censor bars, archive numbering. Reference: Margiela hangtags, Acronym lookbooks, military archival aesthetics.
3. **Industrial typography** — heavy condensed serifs vs. monospace tags. Reference: Pentagram editorial, Apartamento magazine, ACNE Paper.
4. **Body in motion** — model captured mid-movement, mid-jump, mid-fall. Not posed. Reference: Tyrone Lebon, Jason Nocito.
5. **Catalog discipline** — pure white seamless packshots, but cropped tight and printed on dune-tan paper instead of stark white. Reference: COS lookbook, Issey Miyake Pleats Please packshots.
6. **Storm graphic system** — lightning bolt as the only signifier graphic. No sale badges. No SAVE-XX%. Just a single mark that means "new" / "limited" / "archive".

## 3. Visual language principles

### 3.1 Whitespace doctrine
The cinematic hero photo (or video) **occupies 100vh on the home and PDP** with no banners, no popup, no overlay text larger than a line. The next section starts at a deliberate fold, never crowding it.

### 3.2 Typography
Three voices, never mixed within a single line:
- **DISPLAY** — heavy condensed serif (PP Editorial New / Reckless Serif / Migra). Chapter titles, season names, hero quotes. ALL CAPS or sentence case, never title case.
- **TAG** — monospace, small-caps, all-caps (PP Neue Bit / Diatype Mono). Used for: SKU, drop label, size, "Chapter II", "Archive", category tags. ≤11px most of the time.
- **BODY** — neutral grotesque (Söhne or Diatype). Used for: product descriptions, FAQ, checkout, paragraphs.

### 3.3 Color (recap from audit, with hex)
- **Paper** `#F6F4EE` — primary canvas
- **Ink** `#0B0B0B` — primary type
- **Storm** `#1C1C1F` — dark surfaces, drawer, cart
- **Dune** `#C8B69A` — accent, derived from existing hero
- **Bolt** `#C8201E` — signal-only. Used for: new-drop dot, sold-out state, sometimes a brand mark accent. **Never used for "sale price."**

No gradients. No shadows beyond a 1px hairline at 30% opacity. No glows.

### 3.4 Photography rules
- **One subject, one moment per frame.**
- **One light source dominates.** No flat ring-light fashion lookalikes.
- **Movement encouraged over poses.** Dust, jump, blur, walk.
- **Color graded warm, never cool.** Desaturated, but not black-and-white. Dune-tan in shadows, deep ink in midtones.
- **Studio packshots are full-bleed on Paper background**, never grey. Tight crops. Hardware (rivets, eyelets, chains) shown in close-up macro images alongside.
- **Every product has 7+ images:** 1 hero studio, 1 close-up macro, 3 on-body environmental shots, 1 styling/outfit context, 1 detail crop.

### 3.5 Motion system (full spec in Phase 4)
Six core motion archetypes:
1. **Reveal** — Photographic elements fade up + slow scale-down (105% → 100%) at 800ms cubic-bezier(0.25, 1, 0.5, 1).
2. **Marquee text** — Editorial chapter titles slide horizontally with character stagger. *Never* the cliché "KIIKIO.COM KIIKIO.COM" cargo-cult marquee.
3. **Magnetic hover** — CTAs translate ±6px toward the cursor.
4. **Cursor object** — Custom cursor becomes a small lightning bolt or chapter-number disc when over interactive elements. Optional.
5. **Image diptych transition** — On product hover/PLP card, the second image cross-fades while the first scales 1.02 over 600ms.
6. **Scroll-pinned chapters** — Story / lookbook sections use a pinned scrollytelling structure with parallax depth.

Motion budget: max 1 hero animation per fold, all motion durations between 300–900ms, all easings on `cubic-bezier(0.25, 1, 0.5, 1)` or its inverse — *no* spring physics, *no* bounce, *no* overshoot. Premium feels physical and inevitable; bouncy feels childish.

## 4. Tone of voice

### 4.1 Voice principles
- **Declarative, not pleading.** ("New drop arriving." not "Don't miss out — sale ends soon!!")
- **Specific, not generic.** ("Raw selvedge denim, washed twice." not "Premium quality.")
- **Story over feature.** ("Cut from a single bolt of fabric the night the storm broke." over "100% cotton.")
- **Quiet about price.** Price is shown once, clean, no anchor.
- **Sub-cultural fluency.** Speaks to the audience as a peer (Gen-Z streetwear-fluent), not a marketer. No "trendsetters". No "everyone is independent and free".

### 4.2 Vocabulary
**Use:** chapter, dispatch, drop, archive, weathered, returned to the workshop, edition, signal, run, lot, after the storm, the second light.
**Banish:** premium, luxury, trendsetters, worry-free, limited time, hurry, save extra, urgency, "checkout now", emojis (especially ✅⚡🔥 outside of decorative branded contexts).

**Current homepage banner:**

> "Chapter II — Lightning. Dispatching now."

**Current product card:**

> $99.90 ~~$54.90~~ SAVE 45%
> 2 SAVE 10% // 3 SAVE 15%"

> SS26 / Lot 014

## 5. Brand world architecture

Beyond the e-commerce surface, Kiikio post-redesign has four content territories:

1. **The Catalog** — Home → Chapters → Pieces → Detail. Pure commerce.
2. **The Archive** — Past chapters, sold-out items, markdown — accessible but never crowding the front.
3. **The Story** — Founding myth, behind-the-seams photography, designer notes, fabric origins.
4. **The Dispatch** — Lookbooks for each drop, presented as scroll-pinned editorial spreads.

Each chapter ships with all four: a new dispatch, new pieces in the catalog, an updated story note, and yesterday's chapter sliding into the archive.

## 6. Where this lands the brand

If we execute this direction, Kiikio's site will sit *between* these comparables in feel:
- More cinematic than **Eric Emanuel** (which is photography-led but flatly laid out)
- Less monastic than **Aimé Leon Dore** (which doesn't need to convert at the same rate)
- Darker and more story-driven than **Represent**
- Less ironic / Gen-Z-meme than **Corteiz**
- Equally dark but more editorial-disciplined than **Hellstar**
- The premium feel of **COS** without pretending to be quiet/minimalist

It will *not* sit alongside Balenciaga or Fear of God — and that's correct. The credibility comes from owning the right tier with conviction, not from cosplaying a higher one.
